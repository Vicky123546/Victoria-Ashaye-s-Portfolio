04Featured case study — 01

Governance transformation

A firm that advised others on governance had gaps in its own. I led the work to fix that, and then built the system that keeps it fixed.

Done CCC Group · 12 governance domains

From a governance failure to a governance operating cycle

The trigger was a pitch call: the firm was asked whether it was registered with the data protection regulator, and it wasn't. Treated internally as a governance failure rather than an administrative oversight — because the pattern was the problem, not the single question. Policies hadn't been reviewed since they were written. There was no risk oversight, no assurance layer, no RACI, no escalation pathway, no compliance monitoring plan and no clear view of which regulations the firm was accountable to.

8stage governance cycle

My role

Led and executed the transformation end to end: the diagnostic of the current state, the framework design across 12 domains, the technology governance framework, and the design of the platform that operationalises all of it.

Approach

  • Diagnosed the actual current state rather than starting from a template — the existing documents were generic and hadn't kept pace with the business.
  • Made the framework proportionate to a four-person firm with one director, not a copy of a large-institution model.
  • Structured it across 12 domains: corporate governance, risk, regulatory compliance, data protection, cybersecurity, technology, AI governance, people and ethics, third party, continuity and resilience, assurance, and records.
  • Designed the technology arm's governance separately, since it became fully active in March 2026 and carries its own IP and product risk.
  • Built change detection into the design: when something material changes, the framework has to tell you which domains need reassessing.

Evidence

  • A proportionate governance framework across 12 domains, in use by the firm.
  • The Technology Governance Framework, which is currently the only governance coverage for the technology arm.
  • The Governance Operating & Assurance Platform, built to run the framework rather than describe it.
  • Governance technology from this programme applied by an external organisation to develop its own governance framework.

What it developed

How to build a framework that survives contact with a real organisation — one small enough that governance theatre is immediately visible. It also taught me that a framework nobody operates is not a framework, which is the reason the platform exists at all.

08AI security & AI governance

Two questions that have to be answered together

They are usually treated as separate disciplines. In practice, an AI system that fails either one fails both.

AI security

AI security

"Can we protect the system?"

  • Security risks specific to AI systems — how models, data and pipelines are attacked rather than how conventional applications are.
  • Applying existing security foundations — controls, vulnerability thinking, information security management — to AI components.
  • Currently deepening this: the Introduction to AI Security certification is the most recent step.

AI governance

AI governance

"Should the system operate this way?"

  • Responsible AI, accountability structures and who is answerable for an AI-influenced decision.
  • Human oversight as a designed control rather than a stated intention.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 and AI management systems, held alongside ISO/IEC 27001.
  • Emerging AI regulation and what it means for organisations before it binds them.

12About

About

I'm a Technology Governance Analyst and GRC professional working across Nigerian and UK financial services environments.

I started in cybersecurity — a diploma, the security foundations, the labs, the certifications. That gave me the technical grounding, but what kept my attention was the layer above the controls: who decides, who is accountable, and how an organisation knows whether any of it is working. That took me into GRC, where I built compliance methodology, risk registers, gap analyses and regulatory research from first principles.

Technology governance is where those two ended up meeting. Governance frameworks fail in practice for an unglamorous reason: nobody operates them. So I started building the tools that make a framework operable — diagnostics, assurance platforms, decision-support systems, board training. Eleven of them so far. They're evidence of how I think about governance, not a separate career in software.

AI is where all of this is heading, and it's the reason the security background still matters. Governing an AI system means asking both whether it can be protected and whether it should be operating the way it does — and those questions need someone who can hold the security side and the governance side at the same time.
