Featured case study 01

Governance transformation

A 12-domain governance framework designed for a five-person advisory firm, and the operating platform built to keep it running.

DoneCCC Advisory · 12 governance domains

From framework to a governance operating cycle

The programme was commissioned so the firm holds itself to the standard it sets for clients. The design constraint was proportionality: a five-person firm including the founder and managing director needs a framework it can actually operate, not a large-institution model reproduced at small scale. Select a stage to see what it means and how the platform delivers it.

8stage governance cycle

My role

Led and executed the transformation end to end: the current-state diagnostic, the framework design across 12 domains, the technology governance framework, and the design of the platform that operationalises all of it.

Approach

  • Started from the actual current state rather than a template, because the template approach is what produces frameworks nobody uses.
  • Made it proportionate to a five-person firm including the founder and managing director.
  • Structured it across 12 domains: corporate governance, risk, regulatory compliance, data protection, cybersecurity, technology, AI governance, people and ethics, third party, continuity and resilience, assurance, and records.
  • Designed the technology arm's governance separately, since it became fully active in March 2026 and carries its own IP and product risk.
  • Built change detection in from the start: when something material changes, the framework has to identify which domains need reassessing.

Evidence

  • A proportionate governance framework across 12 domains, in use by the firm.
  • The Technology Governance Framework, currently the only governance coverage for the technology arm.
  • The Governance Operating & Assurance Platform, built to run the framework rather than describe it.
  • The governance intelligence tools have been used by an external organisation to assess its governance health across different areas and get visibility into its gaps, so it could build a sustainable framework of its own.

What it developed

How to build a framework that survives contact with a real organisation, one small enough that governance theatre is immediately visible. It also taught me that a framework nobody operates is not a framework, which is the reason the platform exists at all.

AI security & AI governance

Two questions that have to be answered together

They are usually treated as separate disciplines. A system that answers only one of them cannot be trusted on the strength of the other.

AI security

"Can we protect the system?"

  • Security risks specific to AI systems: how models, data and pipelines are attacked rather than how conventional applications are.
  • Applying existing security foundations (controls, vulnerability thinking, information security management) to AI components.
  • Introduction to AI Security certification, applied to the security and control pillar of the AI governance maturity model.
Trustworthy AI

AI governance

"Should the system operate this way?"

  • Responsible AI, accountability structures and who is answerable for an AI-influenced decision.
  • Human oversight as a designed control rather than a stated intention.
  • ISO/IEC 42001 and AI management systems, held alongside ISO/IEC 27001.
  • Emerging AI regulation and what it means for organisations before it binds them.

About

Victoria Ashaye

Technology Governance Analyst and GRC professional, working across UK and Nigerian financial services environments.

I work where cybersecurity, governance, technology and AI intersect. I ended up there because each of those disciplines kept pointing at the next one.

I started in cybersecurity: a diploma, the security foundations, the labs, the certifications. That gave me the technical grounding, but what kept my attention was the layer above the controls: who decides, who is accountable, and how an organisation knows whether any of it is working. That took me into GRC, where I built compliance methodology, risk registers, gap analyses, a PCI DSS project charter, a cybersecurity governance framework and regulatory research from first principles.

Technology governance is where those two met. Governance frameworks fail in practice for an unglamorous reason: nobody operates them. So I started building the tools that make a framework operable: diagnostics, assurance platforms, decision-support systems, board training. Eleven of them so far. They are evidence of how I think about governance, not a separate career in software.

AI is where all of this is heading, and it is the reason the security background still matters. Governing an AI system means asking both whether it can be protected and whether it should be operating the way it does. Those questions need someone who can hold the security side and the governance side at the same time.
